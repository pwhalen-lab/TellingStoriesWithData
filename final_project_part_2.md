# User Research and Interviews 
After developing a first draft of my final project idea, I shared my draft with three different individuals. Since my target audience for my project are CMU students, I chose to interview three current students (MISM, MSPPM-DA, and MAM). I wanted different perspectives, so two of the students I interviewed are part of the Telling Stories with Data class, in two different graduate programs. The other student I interviewed is not part of the class, but I thought this was an important perspective because they are still part of my target audience and have potentially learned different skills when it comes to data visualizations. 

My script asked questions that gave me insight into what worked well, what didn’t work, what made them curious, what they wanted to see more of, and what left them confused or unclear. I started by giving them an overview of the project, and then shared the draft visuals as I went through the key pieces of information. Afterwards, I asked them the questions above.

The first conversation I had, I started to describe the overview of my project. Before even showing my visuals, Student 1, MISM, quickly asked the question “what is compost?.” This was something that surprised me, because I had been operating as if my audience already had a baseline understanding of food waste and food waste reduction strategies. This helped me to understand that my audience could benefit from a short explanation of these things, and when providing them with tips & tricks, I need to make sure to clearly break down how to compost, what someone can and cannot compost, where to find these resources, etc..

Student 2, MSPPM-DA, said the second data visualization I included needed to be more descriptive because it left them confused. They mentioned I should emphasize that the surplus is how much food is wasted, so the audience doesn’t need to do that work themselves. They also gave suggestions around my map idea, saying it would be helpful to label the different points on the map, and specifically highlight where CMU is, and what drop off points are close to campus. I appreciated this feedback because it reminded me that the story isn’t just for the audience, but the visualizations should be created with this audience in mind. What is most useful for them?

In my third conversation, Student 3, MAM, gave positive feedback around my first visualization, but was curious why I was using data from 2023 instead of the most current. While this was because I am using data from a recent case study in Pittsburgh, it was a good reminder to double check that I am using the most up to date data I can find. I will potentially include an explanation of why that is the data I am using. This person also gave positive feedback around the idea of including pictures from compost sites on campus. They were really interested in how composting looks on campus and since CMU students are my desired audience, I believe incorporating data specific to CMU if I can find it will be the most useful. 

As earlier mentioned, the changes I plan to make on the next iteration of the project are to include more detail in the data visualizations to ensure it’s less work on the audience to interpret the visuals. For example, in the second data visualization, I will make it clear that food surplus means food waste. I will also make the story, and the visuals more specific to CMU. This may look like highlighting CMU campus on the map, and identifying compost sites specific to campus. If I can find data specific to CMU composting practices, I will include that. I will also confirm the most up to date information on food waste, and if I choose to keep 2023, I will add an explanation of why that data is being used. I will also include a description of what composting is, and how to do it.
